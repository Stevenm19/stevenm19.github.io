Intro

There exist many methods and intensities that athletes can use to train. From a general perspective, most people think the same two things when it comes to training: Train hard, and Train smart.

But what does that really mean? Obviously, there are different sports and each sport has to train differrently. However, at the same time, each sport can take something away from another. Besides the sport, we need to consider the individuals playing the sport. Even if two players play the same sport, no two players will have the same psychological profile.

Through an analysis of this dataset, we will analyze training methods, adaptation types, intensity level and other factors to answer the question: Which psychological training methods are associated with better outcomes for athletes?

Examining the Training Methods

method_summary <- sports |>
    count(Training_Method, sort = TRUE) |>
    mutate(pct_total = n/sum(n) * 100)

method_summary
# A tibble: 5 × 3

  <chr>                   <int>     <dbl>
1 Goal-setting            13059     26.1 
2 Self-talk               12934     25.9 
3 Mindfulness             12930     25.9 
4 Visualization            7033     14.1 
5 Cognitive Restructuring  4044      8.09

Here, we count all the available training methods recorded in this dataset and calculate their percentage. We can see that the most used training method for athletes is to set a goal for myself. However, this method is extremely comparable to self-talk and mindfulness as well, with all three hovering around 26% of the whole population. This makes sense as all three of these methods are what’s taught in life even away from sports. People are taught since adolescence to set a goal for themselves, to think over their actions, and be aware of their actions and the subsequent results. What’s interesting here, though, is that the lowest used method is cognitive restructuring: a method not very commonly heard of.

Summarizing Effectiveness by Method

method_summary <- sports |>
    group_by(Training_Method) |>
    summarize(
        avg_motivation_gain = mean(Motivation_Gain, na.rm = TRUE),
        avg_confidence_gain = mean(Confidence_Gain, na.rm = TRUE),
        avg_stress_reduction = mean(Stress_Reduction_Percentage, na.rm = TRUE),
        avg_effectiveness = mean(Adaptation_Effectiveness_Score, na.rm = TRUE),
        .groups = 'drop'
    ) %>%
    left_join(method_summary, ., by='Training_Method')
